Which Pages Should We Refresh First?

Ranking content-refresh priorities on ~79M rows of real, pseudonymized search-console data.
A capstone in the FlyRank ML Internship — Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring lane. Decision-support, not algorithm prophecy.

Abstract

A content team that can refresh only a small batch of pages per sprint needs a ranked queue, not a label for every row — so the task was framed as ranking, scored by Precision@K against a defined decline proxy.

Features were built from rolling 30/30/60-day windows of page-day warehouse data, validated with a client-holdout split, and compared against a frozen, human-readable hand rule.

On the dev client-holdout, gradient boosting reached Precision@50 = 0.90 versus the hand rule's 0.56 (base rate 0.546).

On one frozen, sealed out-of-time month (June 2026), the same model measured Precision@50 = 0.80 against a base rate of 0.781, and Precision@20 = 0.75 — directionally useful at mid-depth, weaker at the very top than in validation.

All claims are measured, directional, and decision-support only; the label is a traffic proxy and no causal effect of refreshing is asserted.

1. Introduction — the decision this supports

Thousands of pages carry search visibility; a content team can meaningfully refresh only a small batch per sprint. The operational decision is which pages first. Because the team acts on the top of a list, this is a ranking problem — a scored classifier underneath, sorted into a queue — evaluated with Precision@K rather than accuracy. The output is a weekly refresh queue a human can read line by line.

  • Tables: fact_content_daily_performance (page-day grain; partitions 2025-12 through 2026-06) and the sealed June 2026 outcome window.
  • Verification (measured): the March 2026 partition holds 9,841,378 page-day rows spanning 2026-03-01 → 2026-03-31; 36.7% of rows pass gsc_data_available IS TRUE. The page-day grain was confirmed by a duplicate-key check (zero collisions).
  • Frames: dev — decision date 2026-03-31, 209,493 pages; sealed test — decision date 2026-05-31, 258,233 pages (237,429 with a computable label).
  • Excluded by design: fact_content_query_90d (its fixed 90-day window overlaps any outcome window — a leakage risk), and GA4 engagement columns (mostly unavailable for GSC-only clients; this slice is GSC-only).

3. Methodology

  • Label (proxy, not ground truth): a page is declining if its outcome-window impressions (the 30 days after the decision date) fall below its trailing-30-day impressions. Pages absent from the outcome window count as zero impressions — an explicit assumption.
  • Features: ten candidates from rolling windows ending at the decision date — trailing-30d impressions; momentum 30d/90d; mean position 60d; position momentum; CTR 60d; CTR momentum; impression volatility 90d; days seen 90d; presence share. Observed during the run: position and CTR fields were unreported for most pages without clicks and were absent from the training fold entirely; the fitted models therefore relied on the eight traffic-consistency features, with median imputation / NaN-aware boosting handling the gaps.
  • Baseline: a frozen hand rule from earlier work — visibility floor × month-over-month fall — re-scored on this frame's windows for a same-split comparison.
  • Validation: client-holdout split on the dev frame (a client's pages never appear on both sides): 47 clients, 130,513 train / 45,755 holdout rows.
  • Leakage controls: features hard-bounded to report_date ≤ decision date in SQL; model inputs asserted disjoint from outcome columns, and holdout scores verified bit-identical when outcome columns are dropped; the sealed June window was touched exactly once, after the winner was frozen.

5. Limitations & honest framing

  • One sealed test window (measured): the June result is a single honest out-of-time measurement on a month whose decline base rate (0.781) differs sharply from dev's (0.546); it is not a performance guarantee.
  • Proxy label (defined, not observed truth): "declining" is a traffic rule, not a business outcome. No causal effect of refreshing is claimed; recovery is not asserted.
  • Unbalanced panel (observed): clients entered the warehouse at different times, so identical windows cover different real history per client; features are directional decision-support, not cross-client absolutes.
  • Sparse fields (observed): position and CTR are largely unreported for pages without clicks, so the models leaned on traffic-consistency signals rather than SERP-appearance signals.
  • Small-K noise: Precision@20/@50 are computed on 20–50 rows; small differences at the top of a ranked list are noisy, and rankings — not labels — are the deliverable.

6. Ranked recommendations — the action playbook

The deliverable is a ranked queue at the current decision date (2026-05-31): every page carries a score, a single human-readable reason code (FALLING_HIGH_VOLUME, FALLING_LOW_VOLUME, NOT_DECLINING_SIGNAL), and an action label (REFRESH_NOW / REVIEW / MONITOR). The team works the REFRESH_NOW band first; the queue regenerates from the notebook on every run. Raw row-level output stays out of the public repo by design; the playbook structure and reason codes above are the public, safe summary.
